Security & Privacy

What we protect,
what we can't, and why.

Every messaging app claims encryption. This page explains exactly what that means here, and, just as importantly, what it doesn't.

01

What's encrypted.

All message content is encrypted by default, on your device, before it ever reaches the server. This uses the Matrix protocol's encryption, Olm for one-to-one conversations, Megolm for group chats, the same double-ratchet cryptographic family that Signal uses.

This is not a custom or homegrown system. The implementation is vodozemac, independently audited by Least Authority, with the full audit report published publicly. We chose to build on years of reviewed cryptographic work rather than invent our own.

The server never receives readable message content. This is a structural property of the encryption, not a policy decision.

02

What the operator
can see.

Honesty requires stating both sides. Here's the full picture, what is structurally impossible for the operator to access, and what is visible by necessity.

Cannot see

Message content, ever. The server never receives plaintext. This isn't a policy; it's a structural property of the encryption.

Can see

Account existence, connection timing, IP addresses at connection time, and who is messaging whom (metadata). This is the same limitation every messaging system has, including Signal. We don't claim anonymity, this isn't Tor.

03

Why Matrix, not
a custom protocol.

Was hard but rejected building a custom encrypted messaging protocol. A solo developer designing novel cryptography was a bit hard and didn't have mmuch time. Instead, we adopted Matrix, an open standard backed by years of protocol design, multiple independent implementations, and formal security review.

The encryption layer (vodozemac) has been audited by Least Authority, a respected independent security firm, with the full report published. This is how responsible security engineering works: you build on proven, reviewed work, you don't reinvent it to seem impressive.

Reusing audited cryptography is the responsible choice, not a shortcut ¯\_(ツ)_/¯

04

Where this runs.

Self-hosted on personally owned hardware in Switzerland. Not rented from a cloud provider, not managed by a third-party host. Swiss data protection law (nFADP) applies, one of the stronger privacy frameworks in Europe.

But this needs to be stated plainly: Swiss hosting is one layer of protection, not a magic exemption. If Zmey serves EU-based users, the EU's Chat Control regulation targets the provider regardless of where the server sits. We don't oversell jurisdiction as a legal shield, we treat it as what it is: one factor among several, meaningful but not absolute.

The real protection isn't the jurisdiction, it's the architecture. Even under compulsion, there is no readable content to hand over.

05

You don't have to
take our word for it.

All three repositories are public. The client is licensed AGPLv3 specifically, meaning anyone can inspect what the app actually does, and anyone running a modified or hosted version is legally obligated to share their changes. This isn't transparency theater, it's an enforceable guarantee.
